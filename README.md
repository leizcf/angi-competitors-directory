## The Short Answer: The Main Angi Competitors in 2026

Before going deep, here's the landscape. Angi (formerly Angie's List, now merged with HomeAdvisor under the Angi Inc. umbrella) sits in a crowded field. The platforms contractors most often compare it against:

- **Thumbtack** — pay-per-lead, but you review job details before paying
- **HomeAdvisor** — Angi's sister platform, high-volume shared leads
- **Yelp Ads** — cost-per-click, leans on review volume you've already built
- **Houzz Pro** — portfolio-driven, best for remodelers and design-build firms
- **Porch** — buy leads individually or set a monthly budget
- **Bark** — credit-based, you pick which leads to chase
- **HomeGuide** — quote-based marketplace, customers compare bids
- **Networx** — shared leads, often used as a secondary source
- **TaskRabbit** — gig-style, smaller jobs, IKEA-owned
- **Neighborly** — franchise model, not a lead marketplace

## The Honest Truth About Platform-Hopping

Reading the comparison tables, it's tempting to think the answer is just finding the *right* Angi competitor — the one with cheaper leads or better quality. The reality contractors describe on Reddit and in industry forums is messier.

A plumber on r/smallbusiness summed it up after running ads across Yelp, Angi, HomeAdvisor, Bark, and Thumbtack: *"Yelp has definitely given me the most bang for my buck."* Another said Thumbtack's detailed customer profiles beat Angi's for targeting. A third complained that HomeAdvisor and Angi are essentially the same now, since the two merged.

The Federal Trade Commission even ordered HomeAdvisor to pay up to $7.2 million in 2023 over allegations it deceptively marketed home improvement leads. That doesn't make lead platforms evil — but it's a useful reminder that you're buying a black-box product, and the platform's incentives aren't always aligned with yours.

The contractors who do best on these platforms share a few habits:

1. **They track cost per booked job, not cost per lead.** A $40 lead that closes 25% of the time beats a $15 lead that closes 5%.
2. **They respond in minutes, not hours.** Shared leads almost always go to the first pro who picks up the phone.
3. **They test two platforms at once, not five.** Spread too thin and you can't tell what's working.
4. **They treat the platform as a channel, not a strategy.** Their real moat is reviews, branding, and follow-up — things they own.

That last point is the bridge to the second camp of *angi competitors* searchers — the ones who want to build, not just buy.
